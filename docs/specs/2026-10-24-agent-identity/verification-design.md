# Agent identity and workspace access: Verification Design

How each requirement in [Requirements](requirements.md) will be shown to hold, against the [Specification](specification.md)'s obligations and the [Program Design](program-design.html)'s seams. This is a plan; nothing in it has been observed.

## Proven by: one line per requirement

| U | Your need, in short | Proven by | Stays unverified |
|---|---|---|---|
| U1 | Three kinds of identity | Every workspace and collaboration token and every recorded actor parses as exactly one of the three kinds; anything else is rejected. Machine tokens and service tokens parse only under their own schemas and are refused wherever an actor is expected. | — |
| U2 | Bots prove themselves with their own credential | A bot signs in with an active credential and is accepted, as the same bot through either of two credentials. With a retired one it is refused. Its record shows the storage note. | How real bots store their secrets (accepted limit) |
| U3 | Sessions vouched for by their machine | Two real sessions get tokens through their logged-in machine, each naming itself. After you revoke the machine's login, neither gets anything. | Sessions launched inside VMs (the `Vm` isolation kind): out of this set |
| U4, U5 | Member or collaborator; a bot in at most one workspace | A second membership for a bot is refused. A bot collaborator messages with its collaboration token but can't use tools or start work. A person may hold several roles, and none gives them a token. | — |
| U6 | A session belongs to its project's workspace | Start a real session for a project and see its token name that project's workspace, whatever its repository. A machine not allowed for that workspace is refused, and so are a start with no project and a helper start into another workspace. A conversation whose registration is refused is never prompted. Starting needs no sign-in. | — |
| U7, U8 | Collaborators only message, across allowed pairs | A session messages a member of a paired workspace and it arrives. An unpaired one is refused. Every message, task, board write and Fleet write is checked before it happens, on every path. A pair never lets another workspace write the board, take a role or start work. A bot's message is recorded as from that bot, and a member's reply reaches only that bot. A collaboration only exists where you set it. | — |
| U9 | Look up who belongs where | Each lookup answers correctly for a person, bot, session, workspace, machine (its organization, login state and what authorized it) and pending removal. | — |
| U10, U11 | Members only, within their persona | A member's allowed tool call runs. A collaborator's is refused. A session's wider named persona adds nothing beyond the default. A stricter default applies from the first call served by the deployment that carries it. | — |
| U12 | No live check; configurable lifetime, max 1 h | Tokens stop working at their grant expiry, however late they were minted. The Switchboard answers calls while Fleet central is unreachable. | — |
| U13 | Removals pushed | Remove a member and see its very next call refused once the notice arrives. A token decided before the removal but minted after it is refused; one decided right after a persona change works. With the Switchboard down, or the work interrupted half-way, the removal shows pending and lands later. | — |
| U14 | Fail closed | Stop Fleet central: no new decisions or tokens (machine tokens included), a grant already decided mints only inside its window, collaboration stops, and calls stop at grant expiry. | — |
| U15 | One owner per truth | Every membership change happens only at Fleet central. The Switchboard and sign-in hold no membership. Requests between them come only from the expected service. | — |
| U16 | One contract, any host | The same suites pass for Fleet central on Cloudflare and Tokio, the Switchboard on Cloudflare and Node, and the sign-in service on Cloudflare and Node. The cross-repository joint run is the [delivery orchestration](../2026-10-24-delivery-orchestration/verification-design.md)'s JP1–JP4. | The Node and Tokio hosts in production deployments; the sign-in Node host until it is built |
| U17 | No per-machine keys | No code path on the Router creates or reads a machine private key. | — |
| U18 | Isolation recorded and shown | A session's isolation, with its instance ID and machine environment, appears in its record and token, and its machine's shared bucket lists its workspace. Nothing is refused for isolation. (Kept unless you strike it, OD7.) | Whether the isolation the launcher asserts is true |
| U19 | No false senders | Real Codex (app-server), Claude and Cursor (ACP) sessions trying to post as another session are refused, and stale or foreign bindings are refused. | Same-user processes holding another session's binding (the accepted shared-bucket limit) |
| U20 | People sign-in unchanged | The people sign-in design's own proof, re-run with organization admission answered by Fleet central, and each change leaving its record. | The admin freshness bound SD8 (still open in the people sign-in design) |
| U21 | Only you administer | Your admin actions work and leave their record: roles, allowances, approving a machine's code, revoking a machine. Another member, another organization's owner, a removed owner, a stale session, a bot and a session are each refused with nothing changed or shown. Only the fleet's operator creates organizations. | SD8, as for U20 |
| U22 | Sign-in on Drizzle and SQLite | The sign-in suite runs on D1 and on a `better-sqlite3` file through Drizzle and Better Auth's Drizzle adapter, from a database built only by the checked-in migrations (the device-code table included); concurrent sign-ins and cleanups never delete a claimed person; a scan finds no Kysely, hand-written SQL or `node:sqlite`. | Remote production D1 under load |
| U23 | One sign-in core, injected | The same sign-in suite passes on the Cloudflare and Node hosts, and a run with recording capabilities shows every clock, identifier, crypto and central call went through them. | The Node sign-in host in a production deployment |
| U24 | UUIDv7, good types | Every UUID the sign-in service stored during the suite parses as version 7, no secret or code is a UUID, and the client-assertion replay gate keeps its derived key, so a replay is refused. | — |
| U25 | Deterministic sign-in tests, Hegel | A scan finds no wall-clock read in the sign-in tests; Better Auth's own expiries are crossed with a controlled system clock on the Node host; four Hegel families run. | — |
| U26 | Organization first | A membership or collaboration for someone not yet in the organization is refused; after adding them, it works. | — |
| U27 | Organizations stay apart | With two organizations on one Fleet central, every kind of request that would cross them is refused and reveals nothing; a person in both acts in each separately. | — |
| U28, U29 | Talk only inside the organization; machines see only what they serve | A pair across organizations can't be made. Through every way a machine reads the fleet (directory, snapshot, feed, after a reconnect), it sees only the workspaces it serves and, for paired ones, only their ID and name; adding or withdrawing an allowance changes what it is sent. | What a machine read before its scope shrank (not unread) |
| U30 | People hold no tool tokens | A person's request for a workspace, collaboration or machine token is refused, whatever their roles. A bot's tool call carrying a "requested by" label is decided by the bot's own membership alone. | Who a bot's own channel listens to (outside the system) |
| U31 | Only you log machines in | Your approval of a machine's code logs it in, with its workspaces. Anyone else opening, approving or denying the code is refused, and the code stays for you. A refused attempt leaves nothing behind, at the Registry or in the organization. | — |
| U32 | Anyone at a logged-in machine can start | A start with no sign-in succeeds for an allowed workspace and is recorded as from the machine. | Who is at the keyboard (by design) |
| U33 | One login model | A Router logs in by device code and keeps working past its first token by renewing on its own; two machines you approve stay distinct through refresh and restart, and one machine's replayed old token never stops the other's renewal. Revoking the login stops the next connection and renewal. Logging in again keeps the machine's sessions and allowances, and an old connection left open is refused; logging in after a revocation restores nothing. | — |
| U34 | Three steps | On a test deployment behind a test Access application: step 1 leaves today's login working; step 2 refuses the old credential and every bad machine token even with a valid Access token; step 3, with Access removed, keeps everything working and refuses unauthenticated requests with nothing changed. At no step are both logins accepted, and step 2 has no way back to the old login. | Production's own switch (observed when you run it) |
| U35 | Linux | The Router's login, connection and renewal pass on macOS. On Linux, the secret store's own tests and the memory-only keeping run in codex-router's existing `ubuntu-latest` CI job. | Logging in a real Linux machine end to end, as a systemd service: you declined a new CI job, and no other Linux machine runs it |
| U36 | No role back on readmission | Remove someone, add them back, and see no role until you grant one. | — |
| U37 | One Switchboard per organization | Two Switchboard deployments, one per organization; each refuses the other organization's tokens. | — |
| U38 | No organization room rule | Nothing to prove: no obligation exists. | — |
| U39 | Start a session on another machine | A session on one machine starts a helper on a second machine that serves its workspace, through Fleet central; the helper runs there, belongs to the workspace and names its starter. A start for another workspace, on a machine that doesn't serve it, on another organization's machine, by an ended session, or to a disconnected machine is refused and creates nothing. A disconnect after delivery gives `unknown`, and nothing retries. | — |
| U40 | See, live, who is logged in | You open the who-is-logged-in page and see each of your machines: its approval, its workspaces, connected or not with its last-seen time, and each session's harness, project, start time and whether it is working, idle or unknown. Cut a machine's network and, with the clock moved past the bound, it shows as not connected and its sessions as unknown; close one cleanly and it shows at the next refresh. Anyone else is refused and sees nothing; with central stopped, the page says unavailable instead of showing old rows. At stage C your people and bots appear too. | The bound measured in real elapsed time on a deployed central (the local runs move a controlled clock); how many machines and open pages the 2.5-second loops can carry; how Codex reports a turn starting (the first Router slice settles it) |

## Shared observations

Several rows use the same observation. Each is described once here.

**SO1: Fleet identity journey** (needed, lasting). It belongs in `verify-codex-router` as a new journey `fleet-identity`, because Fleet central owns the truth it reads.

- **What it reuses:** the board-central journey's arrangement of a local central and its cleanup (`references/board-storage.md:3–10, 20–39`), through its TypeScript replacement under `verification/`. The product allows only TypeScript and Rust (codex-router `AGENTS.md:3–6`), so the Python `board_central_journey.py` is neither extended nor relied on, and SO1 waits until that replacement is complete.
- **What must be built:** a native launch part that starts a normal Router (the ordinary production code paths, under an isolated, signed debug Host and Router root on declared ports, never replacing your production Router) with real sessions on each supported path: Codex through the app-server, Claude and Cursor through ACP. The Router logs in through SO8's device-code arrangement. Dependencies: provider accounts and adapter versions on the run machine, the per-session ACP MCP lists through the create and restore commands, and Codex thread `config` (Codex 0.163.0's `thread/start` and `thread/resume`).
- **Not counted as proof:** a captured MCP list without a real Claude or Cursor run, or one provider path standing in for another.
- **What runs:** that Router and its sessions, a local Fleet central (Tokio, plus local workerd) with two organizations, the local sign-in Worker from `verify-auth`'s harness, and the Switchboard through its production `index.ts` host entry.
- **What it drives:**
  1. log the machine in to organization O1 and allow it for workspace W;
  2. start two sessions for a project in W on each path, naming the project, with no sign-in; try a start with no project, a helper start from a session into another workspace, a start for a project in organization O2, and a start from a provider that doesn't support HTTP MCP; withdraw the machine's allowance between creation and registration, and lose a create reply; resend the identical registration;
  3. call a Switchboard tool through the forwarder from each;
  4. try posting as another session, and with a stale and a foreign binding;
  5. send messages, tasks and board writes across a paired and an unpaired workspace, on the Router holding the project, forwarded from a second Router, and as direct messages; with the pair, try a board write, a role claim and a session start; withdraw the pair and retry, including a reply; make Fleet writes as a member and a non-member; try creating a workspace through the Fleet write path; have a bot message a member and the member reply; read the bot's mailbox with each of its two credentials and with another bot's token; withdraw the bot collaboration and reply again; message a session in O2 by its reference;
  6. change a session's persona, then call at once;
  7. end one session (archive) and remove the other;
  8. revoke the machine's login, interrupting its notice outbox and its connection close once each;
  9. stop Fleet central between a decision and its mint, and before a decision, and attempt a collaboration effect;
  10. for Codex, a fresh start, a cold resume, a loaded idle thread rebuilt on resume, and a loaded running thread rejoined across a Router restart (checked from the next post's recorded actor); for Claude and Cursor, each restore mode the installed adapters support; restart the Router before and after a registration reply and during an ambiguous creation, and resend.
- **What it reads:** the tool's effect counter at the controlled MCP server; the Switchboard's refusal classes; decoded token claims; central's lookups, including `RemovalStatus`; board records, stored writes and delivered tasks; the workspaces the Router's local replica holds; and, after each refusal, what that refusal promises (no conversation before creation; an idle, unprompted one after a refused registration; nothing stored or delivered otherwise).

**SO2: sign-in issuance journey** (existing `verify-auth` agent journey, to update, lasting). Its target is the local sign-in Worker on real workerd, D1 and the locked Better Auth 1.7.11, and the sign-in Node host on `better-sqlite3` once built. Changes:

- Bot client credentials take their decision from a real local Fleet central; a bot with two credentials gets the same `principal` through either.
- The fleet-session grant mints for two sessions under central's one client, each with its own `principal`.
- Each refusal class is observed: not a member, identity not proven, machine not allowed, unavailable, and a decision past its redemption window.
- A collaboration token is minted for a bot with no membership.
- Each row of the issuer's dispatch table succeeds through the real provider and the outer release, the machine client's device-code and refresh rows included: tokens are issued under the enrollment's own machine client while the Router device-login client polls, and the provider then refreshes that client. Each refused combination is refused, including a person asking for a workspace, collaboration or machine address, the device-login client being issued any token, and a machine client asking for another grant or audience.
- Credential provisioning returns a secret once and the same credential with no new secret on a repeat; disabling reports `Disabled`, then `AlreadyDisabled`.
- With no per-client resource link rows, an active bot and a session get tokens for their workspace; a collaborator, a wrong workspace, a wrong client kind and an unknown or disabled workspace resource are each refused.
- On the pinned provider, a bot request runs client authentication, central's decision inside the claim contributor, claim stamping, signing, then the release; a refusal or timeout leaves no token.
- Bot A's Basic credentials with a body `client_id` naming bot B never yield B's `principal`, for each supported client authentication.
- **The device-code grant, extended:**
  - **The request and page:** a device request carries the four machine fields, and the approval page shows label, organization and code.
  - **Someone other than the owner:** a member of the code's organization (whose standing comes back through the real typed client as admitted with role `member`), and another organization's owner, each open, approve and deny a fresh code. Each is refused before the plugin's handler; the code's user slot stays empty, and the owner then approves the same code.
  - **Dead codes:** a denied, expired (Node host, controlled system time, at the OD6 lifetime) or used code mints nothing, and an expired code sends nothing to central.
  - **Guess limit:** the sixth wrong user code in a row gets 429.
  - **Single use:** many concurrent redemptions of one approved code, on D1 and on `better-sqlite3`, yield exactly one token set.
  - **Binding through refresh:** two machines approved by the same owner each get their own enrollment and their own machine client, once with both machines in one organization and once with one machine in each of two organizations the owner belongs to; each refresh routes to and stamps only its own organization. Across first mint, several refresh rotations and a sign-in service restart, A's refresh always yields A's token and B's always B's; with A superseded or revoked, A's refresh mints nothing while B's still works. A request carrying a forged enrollment hint changes nothing, because the contributor reads only the token family's reference.
  - **One machine's replay stops only that machine (R40):** in each of those two arrangements, A rotates, the controlled clock passes A's reuse window, and A's retired refresh token is replayed. A is refused. B's refresh rows are read back unchanged, and B's next refresh mints. A build that issues every machine under one shared client fails: the provider's teardown deletes B's rows. Nothing in this case depends on timing, because B's rows never share A's client. Then A's revoked token is presented at the revoke endpoint under B's client, under A's client and under the sign-in app's client: each is refused before the provider runs, and B's rows and the owner's own sign-in refresh rows read back unchanged. A build that lets the provider's revoke handler run for a machine token fails: under B's client it deletes B's rows.
  - **The recorded client:** a `DecideMachineToken` naming a client other than the one the enrollment recorded at its first grant is refused `wrongClient`; a disabled machine client's refresh is refused at the provider.
  - **The window:** a machine grant held past its 60-second window mints nothing.
  - **The minted token:** it carries the machine `principal`, organization and `grant_expires_at`, and its refresh credential mints nothing for any other audience.

**SO3: Switchboard admission** (the Switchboard's existing host e2e suites on the production entry, extended, lasting). It covers:

- each C1 refusal class before any tool runs, read from the controlled MCP server's effect counter;
- persona narrowing, and a stricter default deployed while a token is live, observed on the first call each serving instance of the new deployment handles;
- removal marks: a forged notice, an unauthenticated one, an out-of-order lower one, a duplicate, notices surviving a store restart, and the retention boundary;
- calls answered while no Fleet central exists;
- approvals keyed by principal;
- **one deployment per organization:** two deployments configured for O1 and O2; each refuses a valid token naming the other's organization or workspace; a configuration naming a workspace of a second organization fails to load;
- **a bot's requester label:** a bot token that also carries an extra "requested by" claim naming a person with wider roles gets exactly the bot's own persona.

**SO4: central conformance** (existing `verify-codex-router` central-runtime journey, extended with new named scenarios, lasting). One core on native Tokio and on local workerd, including restarts between each owner's commit and its outbox steps:

- each command's record at its own owner; a command whose record can't be written; the organization's audit view assembled when read; `CreateWorkspace` applied once across a restart and when the owner changes in between; organization creation and owner naming by the fleet operator only; a creation interrupted before the authority is initialized; `AdmitSignIn` and `Standing`; collaboration admission for each operation and refusal class; the credential outbox across restarts;
- **organizations:** two organizations; every command, decision and admission naming the other organization's workspace, bot, session, machine or project answers as unknown and changes nothing; a pair across organizations is refused; one Fleet object per organization (OD8) with the first adopting existing storage, read back after a restart;
- **admission:** a role for an identity not admitted is refused, then granted after admission; removing and re-adding a person or bot leaves no role counting; ending an admission ends its roles (OD5) and writes bot notices; a session's role is untouched by a person leaving;
- **machine login:**
  - **Refusals leave nothing behind:** after a non-owner's approval, an unknown workspace and an unwritable record, both owners are read back: no Registry claim and no enrollment (not even reserved).
  - **Interruptions:** an attempt is interrupted at each handoff (after authorize, after claim, before activate) and resent. It ends with one claim and one enrollment, and a reserved enrollment admits nothing and lapses. The sign-in service resends the same request ID.
  - **Races:** two organizations, each authorized, race for one installation; one claim wins and the other organization's reserved enrollment is cancelled.
  - **Supersession:** a device-code approval stays `approved` until its first machine token, then supersedes the earlier enrollment in one transaction. An approved code never redeemed leaves the working enrollment in force.
  - **Old connections:** connection E1 stays open while E2 supersedes it; then E1 asks for a session token, a registration, a collaboration effect and a Fleet read. Each is refused and E1 is closed.
  - **Refused connections:** a revoked or expired enrollment, another installation and another organization are refused at `AdmitMachineConnection`.
  - **Renewal:** `DecideMachineToken` refuses after revocation. `RenewMachineToken` with a controlled clock keeps a connection past its first token, and a connection whose token expires is closed.
  - **Revocation:** it refuses the next connection, renewal and grant at its commit, writes machine notices, removes the machine's sessions and closes the open connection, across restarts. A later log-in restores none of the sessions or allowances.
- **the filtered view:** a machine allowed for W1 and paired with W2 reads the directory, a snapshot and the feed. It sees W1's records, W2 as ID and name only, nothing of W3, and `Withheld` at every other feed position with its cursor contiguous. Adding W3's allowance sends `ScopeChanged` and a snapshot read returns W3; withdrawing it sends `ScopeChanged`, the replica drops W3, and new W3 entries arrive as `Withheld`. Reconnecting with an old cursor resumes contiguously under the current set. With the Router disconnected, W3 is allowed, W1 is withdrawn, and W4 is downgraded from full to paired, with the cursor already past their earlier records. At reconnect, the first frame is the current `ScopeChanged`, and the replica read back afterwards holds W3's existing records, nothing of W1, and only W4's ID and name. W2's boards, projects and decision records are never sent;
- **remote starts:** a start from a member session of W, for a project in W, to a machine of the organization allowed for W is recorded `forwarded`, with its body and deadline, and delivered once. Two concurrent starts to the same target whose reports return in reverse order each settle their own record, and a report from a machine other than the record's target, or naming a session not bound to it, is refused. A delivery whose acknowledgement is lost leaves the record `forwarded`, and its requester's answer is `Forwarded`, never a refusal. A second registration for a bound record is refused. A registration settles its record as `started` when it binds; with the target's report then dropped and the clock past the lapse, status still reads `started` with that session. A central refusal whose reply is dropped is read back by status, after restarting the requester and central, as the same refusal, with nothing delivered. The same request ID with a changed body is `requestIdReused`. `RemoteStartStatus` from anyone but the original requester is refused. Each refusal is recorded with nothing delivered: a session that is not a member or has ended, a project in another workspace, a target unknown, in another organization, not enrolled or without an allowance (one answer), and a target with no open connection. The same request ID returns the recorded answer. A `RegisterFleetSession` with a `remoteSession` starter is accepted only for a forwarded record to that machine and project. With a controlled clock, a forwarded record with no outcome lapses to `unknown`, including after the organization authority restarts between recording and delivery, and is never redelivered;
- **presence and activity:** with frames injected and each object on its own controlled clock, the Connection object answers `Open` while a frame arrives within each three heartbeats and `NotConnected` one tick past, whether or not its close has run, and the lookup shows the last-seen time as the answer's arrival less its silence; a clean close answers `NotConnected` at once; after central restarts, every Connection object answers `NotConnected` until a reconnect, and a whole-state report restores activity; **no clock difference enters:** with the Connection object's clock set several seconds ahead of, then behind, the organization authority's, a machine cut just after a frame still reads not connected three heartbeats later at the next lookup (a build that compares timestamps across the two objects fails); **a slow Connection object fails the lookup:** one held past the read deadline makes `WhoIsLoggedIn` fail whole, never partial; activity counts only from the connection answered `Open`, an applied report replaces the machine's whole set and leaves sessions it omits unknown, and a report from an older attach sequence is refused; **no reordering loses a session:** with sessions A and B both idle, report 2 (A working) is held before its commit, report 3 (A and B working) commits, then report 2 is released and answered `Superseded`, and the lookup reads both working with no later transition; the same with the first report overtaken by the second, and with report 2's answer timed out, after which the next report converges (a build that applies reports in arrival order, or sends one session's change per report, fails); `WhoIsLoggedIn` changes nothing, fails whole when the Fleet object can't answer, and lists only its own organization's machines;
- **the machine token check:** a valid sign-in service machine token is admitted; a token with valid machine claims whose `client_id` isn't the enrollment's recorded machine client is refused `wrongClient`, both on a new connection and on `RenewMachineToken`; a token from any other issuer is refused; a wrong audience, wrong key, expired token, a token for another organization, a person token, a workspace token and a collaboration token are refused; Fleet central's answer is the same whatever Access identity accompanies the request.

**SO5: deployed check** (existing private Cloudflare deploy cycle, a row, temporary per cycle): a bot token is issued and used; a removal is pushed and the next call is refused; Fleet central is made unavailable and no token issues. Sampling covers arrival and refusal only.

**SO6: contract round trips** (needed, lasting). Fixtures generated from the Rust contract are parsed by the Zod schemas and back, for every shape (including `AdmitMachineConnection`'s `client_id`, the `wrongClient` refusals, and `ReadMachinePresence` with its duration answers) in the Program Design, the machine token claims, `DecideDeviceLogin`, `DecideMachineToken` and their answers included; the removal subject key derived from each side gives the same key for the same identity (F4).

**SO7: service-request negatives** (needed, lasting). For each C7 edge on its real receiving entry, the device-login and machine-token decision edges included: the right caller succeeds, also with a newly rotated key during its overlap; the wrong caller, scope, audience, out-of-scope organization or workspace, malformed body, over-long lifetime, expired credential and withdrawn or unknown key are refused with nothing changed; a valid credential for one operation carrying another operation's body is refused as `wrong-operation`. The confirmation edge's client-authentication negatives and the warm key-withdrawal cases are as before.

**SO8: machine login journey** (needed, lasting). A new `verify-codex-router` journey `fleet-machine-login`.

- **What runs:**
  - a normal Router under an isolated, signed debug Host and Router root on declared ports;
  - a local Fleet central with two organizations;
  - the local sign-in service.

  Clocks are controlled at central and the sign-in service; nothing waits on real time.
- **What it drives:**
  1. `fleet login --organization O1`, which the CLI hands to the Router host, approved by O1's owner through the sign-in service's approval endpoint, choosing workspace W; then an O1 member and O2's owner open, approve and deny fresh codes;
  2. connect, then advance central's clock past the token's grant expiry: central closes the connection, and the Router refreshes and reconnects on its own;
  3. log the same installation in again while the old connection stays open: the old connection is refused for a session token and closed, and the new one keeps the session references, the allowance for W and the board relationships;
  4. revoke the login: the next refresh and connection are refused, and the machine's sessions are removed;
  5. log in again after the revocation: a new enrollment, no session or allowance restored until the owner chooses workspaces on the approval page;
  6. log in by device code an installation already claimed by O2: refused, and both owners read back unchanged;
  7. a Router host started with no encrypted store: it logs in and says the credential is kept in memory; after the host restarts, it asks for a new device code before connecting, and once the owner approves it is the same machine, with its sessions and the allowance for W.
- **What it reads:** what the Router reports; central's lookups (enrollment state, what authorized it, allowances); the Registry's claims; connection admission and refusal classes; the Router's encrypted store holding exactly one credential of the new login after a device-code login with a store, and nothing on disk in memory-only keeping.

**SO9: Router platforms** (needed, lasting). SO8 and SO1's Router-side cases on two targets:

- **macOS:** your Mac or sunclaw, with the data key in the Keychain.
- **Linux:** codex-router's existing CI test job runs on `ubuntu-latest` (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`), so new tests in the Rust workspace run on Linux with no new job or gate: the systemd backend reading its data key from a credentials directory, as systemd hands it over, and the memory-only keeping across a host restart. Sealing the key with `systemd-creds` and the whole login journey with the Router as a systemd service need a Linux machine running it; you declined a new CI job (27 October) and sunclaw is a Mac, so they stay unverified.

Also read on each target: a production build with no secret store keeps the refresh credential in memory only and writes no plaintext.

No VM is a target: proving the Router inside a VM is out of scope (OD10).

**SO10: cutover checks** (needed, temporary per step). The private Cloudflare account gets a test Fleet central behind a test Access application. It runs once per step before production moves:

- **Step 1:** today's login works unchanged.
- **Step 2:** a Router logs in again and connects with machine tokens. Each of these is refused even with a valid Access token: the old enrollment credential, and a missing, invalid, expired or other-organization machine token. An admin and an operator command succeed with no Access identity. Changing the Access identity changes no answer. Two Routers that logged in again exchange a message and a reply between their sessions.
- **Step 3:** with the Access application deleted, SO8's journey passes against the test central. Unauthenticated, malformed and wrong-kind requests change nothing. Repeated failures from one source are limited.

The lasting part of these negatives, the entry's refusals and its request limit, runs locally in SO4.

**SO11: starting a session on another machine** (needed, lasting). A new `verify-codex-router` journey `fleet-remote-start`.

- **What runs:** two normal Routers, A and B, each under its own isolated, signed debug Host and Router root on declared ports, both logged into one organization of a local Fleet central; B allowed for workspace W and A's session a member of W; a third Router in another organization. B creates a real Codex conversation, so the run machine needs a provider account, as SO1 does. Clocks are controlled at central.
- **What it drives:**
  1. A's session lists the machines allowed for W and starts a helper on B for a project in W;

A's connection runs through a local relay that the journey can switch to drop traffic without closing the socket, which is how a cut network looks to central.

- **What it drives:**
  1. O1's owner, signed in, opens the page's refresh route; A and B are connected, each with a session (B's session is Codex, A's is Claude);
  2. a turn starts in A's session, then ends;
  3. B's Router closes cleanly;
  4. A's relay drops traffic, and the clocks at central are moved past three heartbeats with no frame from A;
  5. A's relay is restored and A reconnects;
  6. O1's owner revokes B's enrollment;
  7. the same route opened as an O1 member, as O2's owner, signed out, and called at central's admin route with a bot's, a session's and a machine's token;
  8. central stopped while the owner's page is open, then started again;
  9. at stage C: a person signs in and out; a bot obtains tokens at two controlled times, then the sign-in service restarts; a bot's credential is retired.
- **What it reads:** the rows the page's refresh route returns at each step, with their `asOf`, compared field by field with central's lookups and the Registry's claims; the organization authority's durable state before and after each opening, unchanged; the refusal and its body for every caller in step 7; the page's own state in step 8 (unavailable, no rows), checked once in Chrome as the owner sees it.

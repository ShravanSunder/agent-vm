Organization model

Two steps put anyone to work. First, a person or a bot is added to an organization. Then they join that organization's workspaces, each as a member or a collaborator. A member does the workspace's work and uses its tools through the workspace's Tool Portal. A collaborator only sends messages and tasks to the workspace's members. Fleet machines are enrolled in one organization and allowed to serve chosen workspaces; a Fleet harness session belongs to the workspace of the project it was started for. Nothing is shared or sent across organizations. Where work physically runs (its room) is a separate picture, at the end.

What belongs to what

One example fleet; the names are made up. Organizations contain workspaces, and workspaces contain projects. The member, collaborator and allowed-machine rows inside a workspace point back at the people, bots and Routers added to the organization above them; they are references, not copies.

What the example shows:

  • You are in two organizations (Acme and Personal). People may be; bots and Fleet machines may not.
  • Hermes and Review bot are each a member of one workspace and a collaborator of the other.
  • Each Router belongs to one organization. Your Personal work runs on a different Router (Home VM Router), never on an Acme one.
  • A session is never added by hand. It joins the workspace of the project it was started for, on a Fleet machine allowed to serve that workspace.

Messages reach a workspace's members by two separate routes, both inside one organization:

  • Collaboration. A bot collaborator of a workspace and that workspace's members may message each other, both ways, while the collaboration exists. No workspace pair is needed.
  • Workspace pair. Members of workspace A may message members of workspace B only when the admin has allowed A → B. A pair has one direction: "Product may message Docs" does not let Docs message Product.

There is no collaboration or pair across organizations. People as collaborators are new in your model (the identity design only had bot collaborators); they would use the collaboration route. Proposed.

What this means for the Tool Portal

The Tool Portal's unit stays the workspace: one MCP endpoint per workspace, with that workspace's personas, tool profiles and approvals. What moves is who decides membership: Fleet central decides, and the Tool Portal checks the token it is handed against its own local state. The Tool Portal never calls Fleet central during a tool call.

How example 6 runs, as the identity design draws it:

A bot asks the sign-in service for its token itself, with its own credential; the sign-in service asks Fleet central whether the bot is a member of that workspace before it signs anything.

The identity design already registers each bot in one organization and keeps an allowlist of people per organization. What your model adds:

  • The organization comes first for every workspace role. Fleet central gives no membership, collaboration or token to anyone not in that workspace's organization.
  • Proposed consequence: leaving the organization ends its workspace roles (example 10; you have not decided this). For a bot, Fleet central sends removal notices to each workspace it was a member of, using the removal notices the identity design already plans for a removed bot (designed, not built).
  • People as collaborators. No change in the Tool Portal, because collaborators never use tools. Fleet central needs a way for a signed-in person to send collaboration messages, which the identity design only has for bots.

The thing enrolled is one Router installation, not a computer: nothing is derived from hardware (Fleet framework F-34). Its identity is its enrollment.

What this means: reinstalling the Router makes a new enrollment, and the old one should be revoked. Whoever holds that Router's secret store can act as that Router, so revoking the enrollment is the way to cut off a lost or copied machine. If members may later log Routers in, the enrollment would start as pending until you approve it, and only then connect (proposed, for later).

Where work runs: rooms

Everything above says who belongs where. A room says where an agent physically runs, and so which files it can reach. Rooms are not membership: being in a room grants no workspace, and being a member puts you in no room.

RoomExampleWhat it keeps apart
Open roomLaptop Router running directly on your MacLittle. Agents running as your user on that Mac can generally read the same files
Walled-off cornerA sandboxed agent process on that Mac with fenced readsBest effort, for that agent only, as far as its reads are fenced
Locked roomHome VM Router inside a VMThe guest's files, as long as you trust the host and share no folders into it. It is the only real file wall

The agreed first identity round will have each session record its room kind when it starts, and will show which workspaces share one Router. This is designed, not built. Even then nothing proves the record and nothing is refused because of it (your "record and show now, enforce later"). Enrollment proves which Router installation is connecting. It does not prove which machine or VM that Router runs on, and no identity is derived from hardware.

Still to decide

Your decision: "we can add managers later but it can be a concept, right now its just owner"; earlier: people members "use existing channels or bots to communicate"; "a bot is an existing channel. talk to bots who have access".

  • Who logs a Router into an organization: only you, the owner. That login is the one person check; today it is your operator token. Afterwards the Router acts with its own credential.
  • Who starts sessions: anyone or anything that can operate a Router you logged in, for the workspaces that Router may serve. The system cannot tell who; it only knows which Router. So you control starting by which machines you log Routers in on (machines only you use), what each Router may serve, and revoking a Router when needed. People members never log in Routers.
  • How people members get work done: they talk to a bot that has access, through the bot's own channel. The bot acts, recorded as the bot on behalf of that person (Fleet framework F-30).
  • Managers: a concept for later; they are per workspace (F-29). What they may do is not decided.
  • Not enforced either way: who is physically using one of your Routers.

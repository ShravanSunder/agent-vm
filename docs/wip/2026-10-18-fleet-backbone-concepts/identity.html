Identity end to end

1. **Everything that calls proves who it is with its own credential.** A name, a session ID, a board post's author or a field inside a tool call never counts as proof. 2. **Identity is not permission.** One registry in the fleet backbone says *who* you are. Each product decides *what* you may do: agent-collaboration for boards and messages, agent-vm for tools and accounts, AgentStudio for terminals and files. 3. **Agents are authenticated; personas carry the access.** Each running agent has its own short-lived credential and stays the caller. Its persona's profile sets what it may do, narrowed for that agent and still subject to the resource owner's current grant and audience checks. 4. **Each hop proves something different.** Cloudflare proves *a configured service client is calling*. The agent's credential proves *which agent*. The gateway's delegation proves *for whom it acts*. Your device's signed answer proves *an authorized device answered*; the tool's owner still checks that you may approve. It pulls together the identity decisions from today's conversation, the three Advisors' board threads and the 🦁 Lead's spec. Marks: ✅ **decided** by you (D-number) · 🟡 **proposed**, push back here · ❓ **open**. [Section 7](#today) shows what the code does **today**; everything else is the target. The access rules (workspaces, personas, sharing) are on the [Who can do what](fleet-model.html) page.

2. Who you are versus what you may do

The registry answers one question: *who is this, and are they still allowed in the fleet?* It holds **no permissions**. Each product looks up the registry's IDs and applies its own rules. If the registry also held permissions, one compromised or buggy registry would grant everything everywhere. Kept apart, each product still applies its own ceiling. And changing what an agent may do never means re-enrolling it. - a session ID or conversation ID - the author name on a board post - an agent name inside an MCP tool call - the `X-Forwarded-User-Agent` header - Cloudflare's managed MCP Portal identity for unattended agents: it calls upstream with one shared admin credential

5. How your "approve" is proven

🟡 Proposed. The inbox only carries and shows the request. Who records the decision is still open (agent-vm D3); the tool re-checks it and uses it once. The IDE, Hermes or a web page may *show* the request. Only an answer authenticated as you, from your device or your signed-in session, can decide. A presenter claiming "the owner clicked approve" is refused. The device key proves the answer came from your phone, not that *you* just tapped it. For critical actions you may want Face ID on each answer. Not designed yet.

6. Stable agent, changing credentials

An agent keeps one ID. Its credentials come and go. Revoking at each level stops exactly that level. | You revoke | Stops | Keeps | Status | |---|---|---|---| | one credential | that credential only | the agent, its other credentials, its history | 🟡 | | one agent | all of its credentials | its history, for audit | 🟡 | | a persona | every agent of that kind | their history | 🟡 | | a machine | its services, and new agents from its launcher | history; the effect on agents already running there is ❓ | 🟡 · ❓ | | — (rotation) | nothing | same agent, new credential; an approval still waiting may need asking again | 🟡 | | — (new conversation) | nothing | same agent; no new login, no new token | 🟡 | | — (network reconnect) | nothing | same identity; a new authenticated connection | 🟡 | | — (permission change) | approvals waiting under the old rules | same agent; a new assignment revision | 🟡 | | — (agent moved, authorized) | nothing | same agent; updated environment binding, inside its boundary | 🟡 | | a machine is disabled | new admissions that need it | its identity and history | 🟡 | IDs never depend on token values, key fingerprints, IP addresses or socket IDs. Presence is separate: *registered and offline* is a known identity with an unavailable target; *disabled and connected* is a live connection with access denied. 🟡 How quickly every checkpoint sees a revocation is ❓ (freshness is checked at Cloudflare, at the gateway, at the Mac and before the first effect; the maximum delays aren't chosen).

8. What is still open

Two candidates: send the machine's service token plus the agent's credential, or run a local proxy that attaches the agent's identity so the agent never sees it. Neither has been tried. Which launcher may enroll which persona, how many agents and for how long, and what your consent covers when a persona changes. The maximum delay at each checkpoint isn't chosen. If a checkpoint can't confirm freshness, new actions stop. A copied VM image must not be able to act as the original machine or its agents. No design yet. How Grok or Muse prove *which* agent is calling, and whether they can tie a retry to the same call. Until then they get no approval-gated tools. In one fleet, you as admin, and the backbone, could forge any identity. Fine for a household that trusts you. Not fine if someone needs privacy from the admin; then each person needs their own fleet.

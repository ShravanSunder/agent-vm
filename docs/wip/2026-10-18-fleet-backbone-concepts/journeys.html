Fleet journeys

Each journey follows one real situation through the design: who acts, what each component checks, the outcome, and what isn't built yet. Pick one from the **Walkthroughs** menu, or scroll. The written source is `journeys.md`; the rules are in `concepts.md`. **You** (admin; owner of workspace `shravan`; member of `household`) · **your wife** (owner of `wife`; member of `household`) · `shravan`: your Gmail and GitHub, your Mac, personas `codex-worker`, `mail-assistant`, `chief-of-staff` · `household`: family calendar, household board, household VM, personas `household-agent`, `muse`. Marks: ✅ decided by you · 🟡 proposed · ❓ open (call letter or T-number in `decisions-and-open-questions.md`).

J1. Add the household VM to the fleet

The VM belongs to `household` only. Its files, logins and caches are household's. Household agents run here and nowhere else. Who creates and runs the VM (call E). Clone fencing (T-7). Central-issued machine credentials would be a new proposal (T-10).

J2. Start coding agents on your Mac

Ten agents enroll with no per-agent action from you. Reading Gmail runs without a prompt. Agent credentials in the backbone; launcher authorization (T-5); carrying the agent credential through Cloudflare (T-1). Today agent-vm grants accounts per agent ID (`allowedAgentIds`), not per persona.

J3. An agent sends email and you approve on your phone

One email. A retry of K never sends twice; a second, intentional identical email needs a new K. If you don't answer by the deadline, the agent hears "not approved: timed out" and continues without sending. The D3 protocol (T-3). Authenticated answers in agent-collaboration, which today compares a supplied identity. A fresh-gesture option such as Face ID (T-9).

J4. Grok as your chief of staff

Assumes Grok is xAI-hosted, so it is an external agent (❓ call K). If it runs in your own harness, it enrolls like J2. | Profile | Capabilities | Status | |---|---|---| | Collaboration | read and post on your boards; message your coding agents; request "status" and "summarize"; "take this task" and "start agents" need your approval | 🟡 call F | | Tools | calendar and Gmail read approved; Gmail send requires approval; GitHub blocked | 🟡 | Grok coordinates, reads your calendar and email, and asks your agents for listed actions. Per-call correlation on xAI's platform, so approval-gated tools are unavailable to Grok at first (T-8). Authenticated callers and per-persona board grants in agent-collaboration.

J5. Muse on the household board, talk only

Muse talks and reads; it can't make anything happen and sees nothing outside the household board. To let it ask for events, grant exactly "request add-calendar-event from household-agent" (call F). Everything on that board is visible to Meta. Who of you registers it (call C). Muse's platform authentication (T-8). Per-persona board grants in agent-collaboration.

J6. The household agent uses your shared calendar

Read-only use of your calendar, run on household compute, under your approval policy. What the agent read can appear on the household board, where your wife sees it; sharing into the household means agreeing to that. Revoking the share stops future reads at the next check; it doesn't recall what was read. Not built: shares; Router workspaces as a privacy boundary.

J7. A Mac tool through the cloud, and a lost reply

One effect, and the agent sees the same result. Recovery never runs the tool; a late original reply can't undo a finished result. The whole cloud → Mac path. Approval composition is call I. Direct Mac access during a cloud outage (D1) is unselected (T-15).
